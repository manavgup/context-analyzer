A — The Context Tape

Primary view: horizontal timeline of every block in the context window. Each bar shows a block's lifespan — when it entered, how big it is, and whether it's still being referenced. Stale blocks get dashed outlines and red highlights. Scrub through turns to watch blocks enter, age, and go stale.

Strengths: Most visceral — you SEE the dead weight accumulating. Tells a story over time. Individual block tracking is front and center.
Weakness: Can get visually dense with 300+ turns and hundreds of blocks.

B — The Sediment View

Primary view: stacked area chart where each layer is split into "active" and "stale" portions. You see the context growing like geological sediment — and the stale layer growing thicker over time. Gives an instant "30% of your context is dead weight" read.

Strengths: Best aggregate story — the growing red band is immediately alarming. Familiar chart type. Clean.
Weakness: Doesn't show individual blocks as clearly. Drill-down needed for recommendations.

C — The Health Report

Primary view: scorecards showing context health at a glance — dead weight %, utilization, cache efficiency. Supporting panels list the top stale blocks with sizes and recommendations. Less exploratory, more diagnostic.

Strengths: Most actionable — immediately tells you what to do. Clean, demo-friendly. Easy to scan.
Weakness: Doesn't show the temporal story as well. Less "wow" factor for how context evolves.

My Recommendation

Combine A + B as the two main views with C's scorecards as the header. The sediment chart tells the aggregate story ("look how dead weight grows"), the context tape lets you drill into individual blocks, and the scorecards give the headline numbers. The recommendations from C go in a sidebar panel.
